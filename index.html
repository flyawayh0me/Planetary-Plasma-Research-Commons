Our mission

We are building a future shaped by curiosity, evidence, and shared benefit.

What we are trying to understand

We are a proposed open research initiative dedicated to investigating whether the plasma and electromagnetic environments of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune can be used to generate useful electrical power.

Our long-term vision is a civilization with abundant, clean, reliable energy that supports human flourishing, protects Earth’s ecosystems, and benefits humanity broadly rather than concentrating wealth and power in a small number of hands.

“We begin with scientific investigation, not assumptions. We distinguish established physics from theoretical designs, and hope from evidence.”

Why this matters

The giant planets are laboratories of extraordinary power.

Plasma environments

The giant planets contain extraordinary magnetic environments shaped by planetary rotation, plasma motion, moons, and interactions with the solar wind.

Engineering questions

Whether these dynamics can be converted into practical electrical power remains an open question. We are investigating the physics, constraints, and real energy potential behind that possibility.

Public accountability

We do not promise what we have not measured. We compare this idea honestly with terrestrial renewables, storage, fusion, and other alternatives.

Our principles

We are governed by rigor, transparency, and collective benefit.

Scientific honesty

We publish methods, assumptions, and results wherever possible. We report negative findings and failed hypotheses without ceremony.

Independent review

We invite qualified researchers and technical critics to challenge our models before we claim any meaningful progress.

No hype

We never describe a theoretical estimate as demonstrated output, and we never claim cost-free, unlimited energy without evidence.

Public interest

We believe energy systems should serve the public, not just a narrow class of investors, institutions, or gatekeepers.

First phase

We move from inquiry to evidence.

1

Research and organization

Create a public project repository, compile authoritative datasets, establish research questions, and document what is already known.

2

Modeling

Develop a preliminary electrodynamic-tether model, identify major engineering constraints, and estimate uncertainty and net energy.

3

Independent review

Invite critics, publish the first feasibility assessment, and determine whether further simulation or an experiment is justified.
